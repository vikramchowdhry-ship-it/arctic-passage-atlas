What these feeds are

Independent, public, and dated.

  • Station observations: Environment and Climate Change Canada, MSC GeoMet real-time observations.
  • Imagery: NASA Global Imagery Browse Services (MODIS Terra, MODIS Aqua, VIIRS Suomi NPP).
  • Sea-ice extent: NSIDC Sea Ice Index, daily Northern Hemisphere extent.

What is not here, and why

What the vessel layer is, and is not.

  • The vessel layer uses Global Fishing Watch data under a non-commercial licence and an API token, so it cannot be a public live feed.
  • Gap events within 50 nautical miles of shore are not reliable indicators of intent by the provider's own criteria, and the whole study area is within 19 nautical miles of shore.

Vessels

AIS-reported positions in the Arctic and its approaches.

Two layers. The coloured cells show where ships were seen over the last 30 days across the Alaskan, Canadian, Greenland and Siberian Arctic, from Global Fishing Watch satellite AIS, anonymised and a few days old. The dots are live positions, refreshed every minute: the Baltic Sea and Gulf of Bothnia from Finland's open feed, and the Arctic from the AIS relay when it is connected. No names, identifiers or destinations are shown. This is context, not a tracking service.

Lakes and inland waters

Vessels reported on lakes in and near the Arctic in Finland, Sweden, Russia, Iceland, Canada and Alaska, with lakes that have traffic first. Counts are live. Only the Finnish lakes and Lake Ladoga are covered by the open feed. For the rest, "no feed connected" means this page has no AIS source for them until the AIS relay is set up, and most Arctic lakes have no AIS receivers at all.

How to read it

  • Shipping presence: Global Fishing Watch (CC BY-NC 4.0, non-commercial), satellite and terrestrial AIS, aggregated to 0.2° cells. Source for the live Baltic layer: Fintraffic, digitraffic.fi, licence CC BY 4.0. The Arctic relay, when connected, uses AISStream. These are positions that terrestrial receivers heard. Open ocean and much of the high Arctic have no receivers, so many vessels are not shown. A missing dot is not a missing vessel.
  • The site does not label any vessel suspicious, and it does not call a gap in AIS coverage a dark vessel: a gap has many ordinary causes, including reception, equipment and power. The Vessels layer treats gap events and unmatched radar detections as events with unknown cause.
  • Military, law-enforcement and search-and-rescue vessels are not published. Not for navigation, enforcement or surveillance.
