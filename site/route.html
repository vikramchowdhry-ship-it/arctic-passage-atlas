    Forecasts: Open-Meteo weather and marine models (free service for non-commercial use). The sea-state band is a simple guide: calm below 1.25 m waves and 22 kn gusts, moderate below 2.5 m and 34 kn, rough below 4 m and 48 kn, severe above. It is not a safety assessment. Sun position uses the NOAA method. Ports are marked at their sea approach, not at a berth.

    What “shortest” means here, and what is not modelled
    • This is the shortest path through open water on a generalised 0.1° coastline (about 11 km cells). It is an estimate, not a recommended or safe route.
    • Not modelled: ice, depth, tides and currents, charted hazards, traffic separation, canal rules and fees, piracy and conflict areas, sanctions, vessel limits, port restrictions.
    • Sea ice comes from the latest NSIDC daily concentration analysis (25 km passive-microwave cells), refreshed by the site build. It is one past day, not a forecast, and it is not an ice chart. “Ignore sea ice” shows the geometric shortest path, which can pass through ice.
    • For real passage planning use official charts, ice services and the guidance of the relevant authorities.

    Arctic corridor: what “shortest” means here

    A finer 0.05° × 0.02° grid over the Northwest Passage corridor, with live Environment Canada observations and hourly forecasts for seven communities. It is not a recommended or safe route.

    • Not modelled: ice concentration and type, depth, tides and currents, charted hazards, vessel ice class and regulations. In the Arctic these decide where a ship can go, and they change daily.
    • Use the official Canadian Ice Service products and the Canadian Coast Guard.
