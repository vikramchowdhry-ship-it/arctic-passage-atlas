About this work

Arctic Passage Atlas is an open-source methodology project by Maheep Chowdhary, a second-year Geomatics student at the University of Waterloo. It was built as a technical portfolio and writing sample for GIS, remote-sensing and geospatial-analysis roles.

The author

Maheep Chowdhary

Bio

Maheep Chowdhary is a second-year Geomatics student at the University of Waterloo with a long-standing interest in the Arctic: a region where the maps are sparse, the conditions change quickly and good public data is often hard to read. That interest led him to build Arctic Passage Atlas on his own, as open tools that turn public satellite, weather and vessel data into something a person can explore and check. It is also his technical portfolio and writing sample for GIS, remote-sensing and geospatial-analysis roles, and he maintains it as an open-source project.

The project covers the whole chain from provider data to a published web map: Landsat and Sentinel-1 processing in Google Earth Engine, public vessel and weather data, a route calculator, a tested Python pipeline and a static website. He treats limits as part of the result, so what the data cannot show is published next to what it can.

Enquiries about the method, adoption or collaboration are welcome.

What this project shows

  • Remote sensing: Landsat 8 surface-change screening and Sentinel-1 SAR backscatter, with a documented calibration attempt.
  • Geospatial data engineering: reproducible Python and Earth Engine workflows, configuration in one file, checksummed outputs and an automated test suite.
  • Web GIS: interactive MapLibre maps, live public data feeds and a worldwide route planner.
  • Careful reporting: demonstration data is labelled, limits are stated and no claim goes beyond the evidence.

Purpose

The project asks whether three public-data signals can be brought into one small, reproducible study: local surface change, seasonal ice conditions and maritime activity records.

What it is not

It is not an operational system, a navigation aid, an enforcement product or an assessment of any state, company, community or individual vessel. It does not claim that AIS gaps indicate wrongdoing.

Design choice

The title “Arctic Passage Atlas” is intentionally descriptive. It avoids implying an affiliation with existing organizations that use “Arctic Watch.”
