# Voiceover scripts for reels 11 to 15

Generate each in ElevenLabs (voice: a calm documentary male voice, e.g. Max; model Multilingual v2 or v4), download as MP3, and save as `E:/atlas-reels/raw/voiceNN.mp3` (NN = reel number). Each is about 28 seconds spoken. `python video/build_reels.py NN` builds the reel.

## voice11.mp3 (reel 11)

Why isn't the Arctic already a shipping highway? Ice is the first problem. Even in summer, floes can block a channel, and ships need strengthened hulls or an icebreaker. The second problem is the charts: many Arctic waters are surveyed far less than other seas. And the third is rescue: help can be days away. A shorter route is only worth it if you can sail it safely. That is why watching the ice matters. Follow for one Arctic story, every day.

## voice12.mp3 (reel 12)

Under the Arctic tundra, the ground has been frozen for thousands of years. It is called permafrost. When it thaws, the ground can sink, lakes can appear, and roads and buildings can shift. Satellites can measure this from space. Radar can detect the ground moving by millimetres. Geomatics turns that into maps that planners can use. Even a small town needs to know where the ground is changing.

## voice13.mp3 (reel 13)

Landsat 8 circles the Earth about every ninety nine minutes, at roughly seven hundred kilometres up. It sweeps a stripe of land one hundred and eighty five kilometres wide, and returns to the same place every sixteen days. In the Arctic, the tracks overlap near the pole, so images come more often. That is how I compare the same shoreline year after year, for free. Free data. Open code.

## voice14.mp3 (reel 14)

I study geomatics, and I love city planning. A map can show where a town can safely grow. Where flooding or melting ground is a risk, and where roads or ports are needed. Arctic communities have these questions too, just with sea ice and permafrost mixed in. Planning is about places, and geomatics is how you see them clearly. That is why this project sits between the Arctic and planning.
